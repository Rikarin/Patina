The small PBR forward renderer (plan §3.1, §6 P1). It runs in the Worker: a mesh read by `Vixen.Interchange`, an environment baked by `Vixen.Core.Imaging`, a forward pass written in Raven on the library's `Brdf` and `Ibl`, the library's `Tonemap`, `Vignette` and `Sharpen`, and a readback. It depends on no part of `Vixen.Rendering`. Compiled shaders go through `ShaderCache`, so a Worker's second start compiles nothing.

One camera gives one picture, which golden images and the renderer's tests rely on, and on Apple's GPUs that takes a moment after a mesh opens. Geometry is binned into a buffer that starts small and is sized from frames that have finished, a moment after they finish. A mesh that outgrows it renders differently: with the test fixture's tube of 8,640 triangles at 320 x 200, 13 pixels where it overlaps itself move by up to two levels. Half of those triangles stay below the limit and never change, and a first frame of half the mesh sizes the buffer for the next. So `OpenMesh` draws until eight frames in a row match, since nothing else reports that the size has arrived. This is inferred from those measurements, because Metal reports no such thing to read.

Textures come from a material's images, embedded in the file or beside it, or from pictures laid over the materials by `SetMaps`, which replace a slot and its factor on every part. `ViewportImage` decides what a picture's numbers are: base colour is `Rgba8UNormSrgb`, so that filtering happens in linear light, and its mips are averaged in linear light; a 16-bit PNG or an EXR becomes `Rgba16Float`, so a baked normal map keeps its depth; a float normal holding the direction itself, as a bake's does, is centred on the way in. `ViewportTextures` keeps a texture under its content and what it means, so one file can be two textures, and drops whatever the current materials no longer reach. Nothing here outlives the process: the Server tells a new Worker the mesh and the pictures again.

Tangents are `MikkTSpace`'s and are only made when something needs a normal map, since they read the whole mesh. The shader's frame is the one the baker inverts, interpolated and unnormalised, and the two change together or not at all. Roughness is read from green, metallic from blue and occlusion from red, glTF's channels, so a packed file and a grey one both work. glTF's occlusion strength is not honoured, because Assimp reads it and drops it.

`ViewportShading` is everything the Server says about how a frame looks, and none of it reaches a file. The camera's distance is in framings, so one framing fits the sphere round the bounds into the pane's narrower side whatever its shape; orthographic is the same camera through half a degree from far off, which keeps picking and painting on one kind of camera at the cost of about one per cent of perspective across the model. A view of a stored channel writes the number decoded and draws with no curve, look or effect, so the sRGB encode at the end gives back the file's byte; the tests hold that to one level. The curve and the effects after it are Vixen's passes, each compiled the first time a variant is asked for, and an effect at zero is not run, since Vixen's vignette at zero is not none. A panorama is averaged to about a face texel and read between texels, and Vixen's prefilter reads a mip as wide as each sample, so a sun is neither clamped nor speckled.

Occlusion closes the light as well as the environment. The diffuse ambient is scaled by the map, as it always was; the reflection by Lagarde's fit of the cone a glossy lobe leaves through the opening, so a smooth surface keeps more of its reflection than of its diffuse, and a reflection a normal map turns below the surface goes, by Vixen's horizon occlusion; and the direct light by Filament's micro-shadowing, which leaves an open surface lit as before and takes a closed one towards the cube of the cosine. The shadow map is read with twelve taps on two rings, an edge about four texels wide. None of this reads a channel a model does not have: subsurface, clearcoat and the rest wait on the material model (#142), and a path tracer is its own decision (#143).

Bloom is Vixen's dual-filter chain over the forward pass's light, before the curve, with its threshold at the light the curve takes to white at the exposure set, so what blooms is what would clip. Warmth and tint are gains on the light with their luminance held at one: a grade, not a white balance to a temperature, since Vixen's `Photometry` lives in `Vixen.Rendering`, which this does not depend on. Temporal anti-aliasing is not here, and is not coming (ADR 0031): one still picture of one model is drawn at 4x MSAA when something changes, and a temporal filter needs a run of jittered frames for each of them.

Depth of field is Vixen's thin-lens gather (`ViewportFocus`), run on the light before the bloom and the curve, since a lens spreads light and not the curve's grey. When the lens is open the forward pass resolves its light and its nearest depth apart, and the gather draws the defocused light into the frame's own HDR picture, so nothing after it changes. Vixen's pass is physical and a model has no unit the viewport trusts, so the sensor is scaled with the model as if its bounding radius were five centimetres: the blur at a setting is the same on any model, and it is the shallow focus of a small thing close up, which is the shot of a material. The lens focuses on the point the camera turns round, moved by Focus in bounding radii. A gather of 64 taps makes a disc up to ten pixels wide; wider, a sharp edge shows as copies of itself, so the blur stops there, which leaves a large screenshot a little sharper than the pane. Orthographic has next to none, as a lens would from that far off.

The background colour is what the frame is cleared to, scaled by the share of the environment that does not show, and the sky is added over it, so the two meet in the scene's light and are seen through the same curve as the model. The grid is a pass of lines on the floor the model stands on, tested against its depth and not written to it, with the world's x and z axes; it is drawn after the model and is not in the shadow map. The orbit is free over the poles: up is the way the eye moves as the pitch grows, so past straight down the camera turns over rather than spinning round the axis. The texel density view measures texels per unit at each pixel, from how much of the map and how much of the surface the pixel covers, against the project's density, at the size of the map a material wears.
